# Module: radio

Report the radio work in the transcript.

Look for:

- The calls the pilot makes, and to whom, in the order they are made.
- Readbacks: whether an instruction or a clearance is read back, and whether the
  readback matches what was said.
- Phraseology: calls that are notably crisp and complete, and calls that are
  hurried, run together, or missing a piece.
- Calls that come at a busy moment, and calls that are made early and calmly.
- Anything said by another station that clearly concerns this aircraft.

Work from the transcript. Quote the words as they appear, briefly, when the
wording is the point. If a segment is garbled in the transcript, say it is
unclear rather than guessing what was said. Cockpit intercom and radio often
sound the same to a transcriber, so mark a call as `inferred` when you are not
certain it went over the air.

Every observation still needs a frame timestamp, so cite the timestamp closest
to the moment in the transcript.

Do not correct the phraseology. Report it. Good radio work is a `skill`
observation and worth naming.
