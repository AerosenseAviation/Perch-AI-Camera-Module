# Module: attitude

Report what the horizon in the frames shows about pitch and bank.

Look for:

- The steepest bank of this phase, and how it was entered and rolled out of.
- Whether the wings return to level cleanly or hunt either side of level.
- The longest steady wings-level run in this phase, and how steady it is.
- Pitch changes that stand out: a nose that drops or rises noticeably, a
  deliberate attitude change, a moment where the horizon leaves the frame.
- Whether the horizon behaves smoothly or in a series of small corrections.

Judge bank from the angle of the horizon against the frame, and pitch from where
the horizon sits vertically in the frame. Both are relative to the camera, and
the camera is not the aircraft: say "the horizon rolls to about a third of the
way to vertical" rather than naming a bank angle in degrees. Do not compute
angles. If the camera is body-worn or head-worn, remember that the image moves
with the pilot, not with the aircraft, and say so when it matters.

Steep, tidy, or unusual attitudes are `character` or `skill` observations and
are worth reporting for their own sake.
